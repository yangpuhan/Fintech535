What I read off this

1. Where is the cloud dense, and where is it empty?
Dense in a narrow vertical band within a couple of dollars of spot, and in the front one or two expiries — that is where both a mark and a real print exist. It thins out in two directions at once: away from spot, where marks survive but trades stop, and further out in time, where entire rows of the grid go grey. On the busiest session in this window, 208 of 226 listed call cells carried a mark but only 114 of them ever traded — so roughly half the surface is a number nobody transacted at.
2. Why is interpolating across empty cells dangerous on a $0.50 grid for UUUU?
UUUU trades in the $8–15 range, so consecutive $0.50 strikes are only three to six percent apart in moneyness, and option value across strikes is curved rather than straight — a straight line through the most curved part of the payoff is exactly the wrong shape. It gets worse on the wings: out there a missing cell does not mean “price unknown”, it means there is no bid at all, and the interpolator will still hand back a confident positive number for a contract the market is valuing at zero. The sheet looks smoothest precisely where it knows least.
3. Which field is the mark next week, and which is evidence someone traded?
THEO_VALUE is the mark. It is the only field that prints on the contracts nobody trades, which is exactly where a price is needed, and it moves with the underlying rather than going stale. TRDPRC_1 is evidence and nothing more: one trade, at one moment in the session, on a series that may not trade again for a week. Taking it as a mark means pricing a whole surface off a single stale print.
A note on fields. The brief names SETTLE as the exchange mark. That field does not exist on this universe — LSEG answers “the universe does not support the following fields: [SETTLE]” for OPRA equity options, which is why the original cache came back holding nothing but TRDPRC_1. THEO_VALUE is the substitute and it plays the role the brief describes: a model-ish number that prints on more series than trades do. BID and ASK came along too, so the fill question below has a real answer.

The mark is not the trade

Two different series, plotted against each other. If they were the same number every point would sit on the dashed line.

So what would you actually get filled at?

The honest answer is a range, not a number. Where the band is tall the mark is a guess, and where the bid drops to zero there is nobody on the other side.
