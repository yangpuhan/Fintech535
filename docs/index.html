The Wheel

Sell cash-secured puts; if assigned, sell covered calls on the shares; if called away, start again. AAPL, every session from January 2022 to September 2025, a $100,000 cash account, fills measured on the tape rather than assumed.

Thesis, then result

    01Why it should work

    The wheel is two trades that are almost the same trade. A cash-secured put is cash plus a short put; a covered call is shares plus a short call; by put–call parity, at the same strike and expiry those are the same position. So whichever leg it is on, the wheel is short one option against fully paid collateral, all the time. That gives three reasons it should beat holding the stock on a risk-adjusted basis, and one reason it should trail it on raw return.

    The premise, tested on its own: do AAPL options cost more than the moves they insure?

    On the first session after each monthly expiry, the rule's 0.30-delta put on the next monthly: its implied volatility, against the volatility AAPL then actually realised until that expiry. No strategy, no path — one put and one call a month, held to expiry.

    02The rules

    Fixed before the backtest ran, at textbook wheel settings (0.30 delta, about a month to expiry, calls never struck below the cost basis). No discretion anywhere: the same tape always produces the same blotter. Section 07 shows other settings.

    Timing
    Decide on the close of session t−1, fill on the close of t. Every input to a decision — spot, quotes, delta, cash — is from the previous close. Exchange events (expiry, assignment) happen on the close they happen on. Daily bars only give closing quotes; deciding and filling on the same print would be look-ahead. Section 08 tests this directly.
    Put leg
    With no shares and no option open: sell puts on the first monthly expiry with ≥ 21 days left (in practice 27–35), at the strike with delta closest to −0.30. As many contracts as the cash secures: n = ⌊cash ÷ (100 × strike)⌋. Every put is fully cash-secured; nothing is borrowed.
    Assignment
    Put in the money at the expiry close → buy 100 shares per contract at the strike. The shares' cost basis is the strike less that put's premium, and falls by every call premium collected on them afterwards. Earlier puts that expired or were closed are not in it: they were income on cash, before the shares existed.
    Call leg
    With shares and no option open: sell one call per 100 shares, first monthly with ≥ 21 days, at the strike with delta closest to 0.30 among strikes at or above the cost basis — the textbook "never sell a call below what you paid". If no such strike has a bid, hold the shares uncovered and try again next session.
    Called away
    Call in the money at the expiry close → deliver the shares at the strike. Back to cash, back to puts the next session.
    Roll
    Profit-take: once an open option can be bought back for half of what it was sold for (closing mid ≤ 50% of the premium), buy it back and sell the next one by the same rule in the same order, on a later expiry than the one closed. Otherwise hold to expiry. This is the standard mechanical wheel management; the hold-to-expiry wheel is run on the same tape as a control.
    Early exercise
    Call: on the session before an ex-dividend date, in the money with less time value than the dividend → assigned. Put: in the money with less time value than the interest on the strike to expiry, K(1 − e−rT) → assigned. Both are when the holder is better off exercising; this happened .
    Fills
    Sell at mid − × half-spread, buy at mid + × half-spread. The is measured, not chosen — section 03. A sale needs a bid above zero; no two-sided quote, no fill, decide again next close. Shares change hands only on assignment, at the strike.
    Cash
    Idle cash — including the cash securing a put — earns the 3-month T-bill rate (US3MT=RR, over the window), actual/360. That is how a cash-secured put is held in practice: in a sweep or in T-bills. Dividends are credited on the ex-date to shares held into it.
    Delta
    Black–Scholes from the closing mid, the T-bill yield and the trailing dividend yield — the same as 1.3. Agrees with LSEG's DELTA field to a median of where both exist.
    Controls
    All on the same $100,000, same tape, same fills. Put-write: the put leg forever — an in-the-money put is settled in cash and the next put sold, never taking shares (CBOE PUT-style). Buy-write: the call leg forever — shares bought on day one, and when a call is assigned the shares are bought straight back on the same close (CBOE BXM-style; 1.3's covered call). Buy & hold: as many shares as $100,000 buys on the wheel's first day. T-bills: the cash alone.

          08Checks

          No look-ahead: delete the future, the past must not change

          tape cut aftertrades up to thenidentical to the full run

          Every price series — stock, options, rates — is truncated at the date and the whole book re-run from scratch. If any rule used later data, the earlier trades would come out different. The calendar and expiry dates are kept (published years ahead), and dividends up to the next ex-date (AAPL declares them a week or more before).
