Poor Man's Covered Call

Swap the 100 shares under a covered call for one deep in-the-money LEAPS call, keep writing the same calls against it, and measure what the capital saving actually buys. AAPL, every session from January 2022 to October 2026.

Short answer

    01What the strategy is

    A covered call is 100 shares plus one short call. The PMCC replaces the shares with a long-dated call deep in the money, which moves roughly like the stock but costs a fraction of it. Technically it is a diagonal call spread. Below: the book's first trade, valued at the short call's expiry.

      02The rules

      Fixed before the backtest ran, at textbook PMCC settings (0.80-delta long, 0.30-delta short, 30–45 days). No discretion anywhere: the same tape always produces the same blotter. Section 06 shows what happens with other settings.

      Timing
      Decide on the close of session t−1, fill on the close of t. Every input to a decision — spot, quotes, delta, the T-bill rate — is from the previous close. Daily bars only give closing quotes, so deciding and filling on the same print would be look-ahead. Section 07 tests this directly.
      Long leg
      Buy 1 call on the nearest January expiry with ≥ 300 days left, at the listed strike with delta closest to 0.80. When it has ≤ 120 days left, sell it and buy the next one by the same rule, in one order. Never re-struck otherwise. Six LEAPS over the window, Jan-2023 to Jan-2028.
      Short leg
      Sell 1 call on the first monthly expiry with ≥ 21 days left (typically 25–35), at the strike with delta closest to 0.30 that passes the gap rule. Hold to expiry: no profit-taking, no rolling.
      Gap rule
      Kshort − Klong ≥ midlong − midshort, on the decision close. If AAPL gaps through the short strike, the spread is worth at least its width. The rule makes the width cover what the spread costs today, so a rally can never lose money. It is the textbook "short strike above long strike plus net debit", applied to current marks rather than the original cost. It also keeps Kshort above Klong, so the short call is always covered and the margin requirement is just the debit.
      Expiry
      Close ≤ K: expires worthless. Close > K: assigned, settled at intrinsic (S − K) × 100 — deliver at K and buy back on the same close. A new short is decided that evening and sold the next session.
      Early exercise
      On the session before an ex-dividend date, a short call in the money with less time value than the dividend is assumed exercised, settled at intrinsic. This happened once, 9 May 2024.
      Fills
      Options at the closing mid, (BID + ASK) / 2; a sale needs BID > 0. Stock at the closing print. No two-sided quote → no fill, decide again next close. Results are also shown paying the half-spread on every option fill.
      Combo
      No short call without a long call under it. If the first long can't fill, neither does the short.
      Delta
      Black–Scholes from the closing mid, with the 3-month T-bill yield and the trailing 12-month dividend yield. LSEG's own DELTA field is blank on hundreds of quotes in early 2022, so a rule that relied on it could not trade on day one. Ours agrees with LSEG's to a median of where both exist.
      Benchmarks
      Covered call: 100 shares bought on the PMCC's first fill, plus exactly the same short calls — same days, strikes and fills. So every dollar between the two is the long leg. Stock: the shares alone. LEAPS only: the long leg alone. Dividends are credited to share-holding books on the ex-date.
      Capital
      Cash accounts, no borrowing. Capital required is the smallest opening balance that pays for every trade. For the like-for-like comparison every book gets the same , the largest requirement of the four, and idle cash earns the T-bill rate.

        07Checks

        No look-ahead: delete the future, the past must not change

        tape cut aftertrades up to thenidentical to the full run

        Every price series — stock, options, rates — is truncated at the date and the whole book re-run from scratch. If any rule used later data, the earlier trades would come out different. The exchange calendar and expiry dates are kept (they are published years ahead), and dividends up to the next ex-date (AAPL declares them a week or more before).

        Data and accounting

          Not modelled. Commissions (at $0.65 a contract the PMCC's option fills cost about ). Fills better or worse than the closing quote — the two-leg orders are assumed to fill at both mids on the same close. Early exercise for any reason other than a dividend. Pin risk at expiry. Taxes. Buying the LEAPS on margin (Reg T allows 75% financing past nine months; this book pays in full). And the choice of AAPL itself: it was picked as a deep, liquid options market, but it is also a stock that rose in the window, which flatters any long-delta book. The comparison between books is the fair test, not the level.

          09What I would test next

          • Other stocks and a flat market. One name in one window that rose 85% is one observation. Call-writing is built for sideways markets; this window was mostly a strong uptrend.
          • Managing the short call. This book holds every call to expiry, by design, so the rule stays simple. Rolling up and out when a call is tested, or closing at 50% of the premium, are the standard alternatives and the obvious next experiment.
          • A shorter long leg when rates are high. The financing charge grows with the rate times the time to expiry. With T-bills at 4–5%, a 6–9 month long carries less of it, at the cost of more frequent rolls and more spread paid.
          • Intraday fills. Daily closes cannot show how two legs fill as one order. A year of hourly data exists and would test the mid-fill assumption the way 1.2 did.
