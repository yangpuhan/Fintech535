Earnings Surprise Drift

When one of the twenty largest U.S. companies moves more on its earnings than its own options had priced, the market has under-reacted: the stock keeps drifting the same way, relative to the market, for about a month.

Loading the tracker…

01The thesis

Before every report the options market states how far it expects the stock to move: the price of the at-the-money straddle (a call plus a put at the strike nearest the share price), divided by the share price. A reaction bigger than that is news the market did not expect. Large surprises are absorbed slowly: analysts revise their estimates over the following weeks, funds that hold billions of a name cannot rebuild or cut a position in a day, and many investors anchor on the old price. So the first day's move is not the whole repricing.

The mispriced risk is that continuation. On the reaction day the stock is priced as if the move were finished; the thesis is that, after a move bigger than the options priced, the next weeks carry a predictable drift in the same direction. It is traded with shares, hedged with SPY, so what is left is the company news, not the market.

02Time horizon

  • Each trade lasts 20 sessions, about four weeks. That covers the round of estimate revisions and the slow repositioning of large holders after a report, and ends well before the next quarter's news starts to build.
  • About 29 trades a year, in the earnings seasons: late January to February, April to May, July to August, October to November. December is quiet; in 2026 only Oracle and Broadcom report then.
  • The edge should last as long as investors keep digesting big surprises slowly. It is a behavioural pattern, not a structural one, so it can fade or go missing for a while: in this data it failed in 2022 and again in 2026. The conditions in section 04 decide, each night, whether it still holds.

03The tracker

For every earnings report by a company in that year's top 20: the move the options priced (straddle ÷ share price at the last close before the report) against the move on the reaction day. A move beyond the options' estimate is a signal. The tracker follows each signal for 20 sessions from the next open, net of SPY and of costs, and adds up the results in the order they were taken. The grey line does the same for reports that stayed within the options' estimate. If the thesis is right the green line rises and the grey one does not. The tracker follows every signal, paused or not, because it is the evidence for the thesis. The lower panel is the average of the last 20 closed trades, the first condition in section 04.

04Wrong if

Three numbers, recomputed every night from the tracker. The first one only pauses new trades; the other two would mean the thesis is wrong.

05Alpha and costs

  • Costs. 5 bp a side on the stock and on SPY, 20 bp a round trip, charged on every trade. The large caps here trade at a fraction of that spread; the margin covers a worse fill at the open after a big gap.
  • Turnover. About 29 round trips a year, each a stock leg and an SPY leg.
  • Market exposure. Every position carries an equal and opposite SPY position, so the book's beta to the market is about zero.
  • How rough. The average trade is positive, but the median is about zero: a few large moves carry the result, and with this many trades the edge could be smaller than shown. Idle cash in a T-bill ETF is about a third of the backtest's return.

06Macro environment

The thesis is about how fast investors absorb company news, so it works best when company news is what moves stocks. In the rising market of 2023 to 2025 it did; in 2022, when rate hikes drove everything, and in the flat, choppy market of 2026, the market kept overriding the company signal and the drift did not hold. Mid-November 2026 is that kind of tape: SPY fell about 9% between 11 and 20 November and the VIX closed near 23 on the 16th, and the first condition above has the rule standing aside. Rates matter less for the signal than for the cash: the 3-month T-bill yields about 4.2% (LSEG, mid-November 2026), and a Fed cut lowers what idle cash earns without touching the trades. A tech selloff matters more: most of the top 20 are technology companies, so a sector-wide move is not hedged by SPY, and it raises the number of big surprises while making the drift after them less reliable. Credit shows no stress for now: the Baa corporate spread over Treasuries was 1.56 percentage points on 19 November (FRED), just under its 200-day average of 1.60.

07The rule and the nightly run

Universe
The 20 largest S&P 500 companies by market cap on the first session of each year, one share class each.
Signal
The close-to-close move into the reaction day is larger than the straddle ÷ share price at the last close before the report (first weekly expiry after the reaction day, at the mid). The reaction day is the next session for a report after the close, the same day for one before the open.
Trade
At the next open, buy the stock after a move up or short it after a move down, with an equal and opposite SPY position. Close both at the open 20 sessions later.
Size
25% of equity a position, at most four at full size; above four, every position scales down so the stocks never exceed equity. Idle cash sits in a T-bill ETF.
Every night
The GitHub Action pulls the last week of daily bars for the top 20 and SPY, the 3-month T-bill yield and the report dates from LSEG. For a report whose reaction day has closed it prices the straddle (two requests), records the row in drift/data/events.csv, and rewrites docs/drift/tracker.json, which this page draws.
Data: LSEG (daily bars, report dates, weekly option quotes, S&P 500 constituents and market caps, 3-month T-bill yield) · FRED (Baa spread) · CBOE (VIX). Backtest and tracker from Jan 2022.
