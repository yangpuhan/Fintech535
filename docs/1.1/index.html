What I read off this

1. Where is the cloud dense, and where is it empty?
Dense in a narrow vertical band within a couple of dollars of spot and in the front one or two expiries — that is the only region where a mid and a real trade both exist. It empties in two directions at once: away from spot, where mids survive but trades stop, and further out in time, where whole rows of the grid go grey. On the busiest session in this window, 226 call cells were listed, 150 carried a closing mid, and only 114 traded — 63% of the grid never changed hands. The surprise is at the edge: 30 of those trades have no mid at all, because by the close the bid had gone away and there was nothing left to take a midpoint of.
2. Why is interpolating across empty cells dangerous on a $0.50 grid for a name like UUUU?
UUUU trades in the $8–15 range, so consecutive $0.50 strikes sit only three to six percent apart in moneyness, and option value across strikes is curved rather than straight — running a straight line through the most curved part of the payoff is exactly the wrong shape, and it biases low near the money. On the wings it stops being a shape problem and becomes a meaning problem: out there a missing cell does not say "price unknown", it says there is no bid at all, and the interpolator will still hand back a confident positive number for a contract the market prices at zero. The sheet looks smoothest precisely where it knows least.
3. Which field is the mark next week, and which is evidence that someone traded?
MID_PRICE is the mark. It is the field that survives on the contracts nobody trades, which is exactly where a price is needed, and being a midpoint of live quotes it moves with the underlying instead of going stale. TRDPRC_1 is evidence and nothing more: one trade, at one moment in the session, on a series that may not trade again for a week. Treating it as a mark means pricing a whole surface off a single stale print. But the mid is not free of sin either — on a barely-traded name it is the middle of a quote that may itself be stale, and the bid/ask band below shows how wide that middle really is.
On the mark field. There is no exchange settlement price here. LSEG rejects SETTLE on this RIC space with "the universe does not support the following fields", and TR.SettlementPrice comes back empty, so MID_PRICE — the closing NBBO midpoint — is the closest mark-of-the-close available. It is still a model-free number rather than a traded one.

The mid is not the trade

Two different series plotted against each other. If they were the same number, every point would sit on the dashed line.

So what would you actually get filled at?

The honest answer is a range, not a number. Where the band is tall the mid is the middle of a guess, and where the bid drops to zero there is nobody on the other side at all.
